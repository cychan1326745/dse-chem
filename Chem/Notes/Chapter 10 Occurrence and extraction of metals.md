# Chapter 10 Occurrence and extraction of metals
## 10.1 Occurrence and uses of metals
### Key definitions
1. **Mineral**: Naturally‑occurring solid inorganic compounds found in the Earth’s crust.
2. **Ore**: A mineral deposit from which a metal can be extracted economically and profitably.

### Occurrence states of metals
1. **Native state (free element)**
Metals with very low reactivity exist as uncombined elements, e.g. gold (Au), platinum (Pt). They hardly react with oxygen, water in nature.
2. **Combined state**
Most reactive and moderately reactive metals exist as compounds (mainly oxides, sulfides, carbonates) in ores.
Highly reactive metals (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al) always exist in combined state; moderately reactive metals (Zn, Fe, Pb) also exist in combined state; copper and silver can be found in both states.

### Relationship between reactivity and occurrence
The lower the reactivity of a metal, the higher the chance it can be found in native state. Highly reactive metals easily form compounds, so only exist in combined state.

#### Explanation linking properties to uses (DSE exam keywords)
‑Aluminium: Low density, good corrosion resistance → suitable for aircraft manufacture, even though its relative strength is not the highest.
‑Copper: Very good electrical conductivity → used for electrical wires, despite its relatively high cost.
‑Iron: High strength and cheap price → widely used in construction, but it rusts easily (low corrosion resistance).
‑Silver: Best electrical conductivity among the four metals, but very expensive, only used for specialised electronic parts instead of general wiring.

## 10.2 Extraction of metals from their ores
### Core principle
Metal extraction is a **reduction process**: metal cations in ores gain electrons and are reduced to metal atoms.

### Extraction methods determined by metal reactivity series
Reactivity series: $K>Na>Ca>Mg>Al>Carbon>Zn>Fe>Pb>(H)>Cu>Ag>Au$

#### 1. Extraction of less reactive metals by heating sulphide ores in air followed by further reduction
##### (1) Thermal decomposition of metal oxide
General equation: metal oxide $\xrightarrow{heat}$ metal + oxygen
Word equation: silver oxide $\xrightarrow{heat}$ silver + oxygen
Balanced chemical equation:
$$2Ag_2O(s)\xrightarrow{\Delta} 4Ag(s)+O_2(g)$$

##### (2) Extraction of mercury from cinnabar (mercury(II) sulphide)
Word equation: mercury(II) sulphide + oxygen $\xrightarrow{heat}$ mercury + sulphur dioxide
Balanced chemical equation:
$$HgS(s)+O_2(g)\xrightarrow{\Delta}Hg(l)+SO_2(g)$$

##### (3) Extraction of lead: first roast lead(II) sulphide ore in air
Word equation: lead(II) sulphide + oxygen $\xrightarrow{heat}$ lead(II) oxide + sulphur dioxide
Balanced chemical equation:
$$2PbS(s)+3O_2(g)\xrightarrow{\Delta}2PbO(s)+2SO_2(g)$$

##### (4) Reduce lead(II) oxide with carbon
Word equation: lead(II) oxide + carbon $\xrightarrow{heat}$ lead + carbon dioxide
Balanced chemical equation:
$$2PbO(s)+C(s)\xrightarrow{\Delta}2Pb(l)+CO_2(g)$$

#### 2. Reduction by heating with carbon / carbon monoxide (moderately reactive metals: Zn, Fe, Pb)
![[Ch10_002.png]]
This is the cheap industrial method. Metal oxides are reduced by coke (carbon) or carbon monoxide at high temperature.
Example: Extraction of iron in blast furnace (haematite $Fe_2O_3$ as iron ore)
Raw materials: haematite, coke, limestone

Word equation: iron(III) oxide + carbon monoxide $\xrightarrow{high\;temperature}$ iron + carbon dioxide
Balanced chemical equation:
$$Fe_2O_3(s)+3CO(g)\xrightarrow{high\;temperature}2Fe(l)+3CO_2(g)$$
‑CO acts as reducing agent
‑Limestone decomposes to remove silica impurity ($SiO_2$) and form slag.

#### 3. Electrolysis of molten ore (for highly reactive metals: K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al)
Metals above carbon cannot be reduced by carbon. Their oxides only can be reduced by electrolysis of molten compounds. The process consumes large amounts of energy and costs much.
Example: Extraction of aluminium from bauxite ($Al_2O_3·2H_2O$)
Cryolite is added to lower the melting point of aluminium oxide to save energy.

Word equation: molten aluminium oxide $\xrightarrow{electricity}$ aluminium + oxygen
Overall electrolysis balanced equation:
$$2Al_2O_3(l)\xrightarrow{electrolysis}4Al(l)+3O_2(g)$$

#### 4. Thermal decomposition / physical separation (very unreactive metals: Ag, Au)
Silver oxide decomposes into silver and oxygen upon heating. Gold exists as native metal and can be separated by physical mining directly without chemical reaction.

## 10.3 Relating the discovery of metals with the ease of extraction of metals
‑Metals that are easy to extract (low reactivity, can be separated by physical method or simple heating) were discovered earlier in human history, e.g. gold, copper, iron.
‑Highly reactive metals (Al, Na, K) require electrolysis for extraction, which needs advanced electricity technology, so they were discovered much later.

General rule: The lower the reactivity of a metal, the easier its extraction, the earlier it was discovered by humans.

Common DSE exam point:
Aluminium is abundant in the Earth’s crust but was discovered late because extracting aluminium needs electrolysis, which was not available in ancient times.

## 10.4 Conserving metals
Metals are finite non‑renewable resources; metal ores will be used up gradually. Methods to conserve metals:
1. **Recycle metals**
Advantages:
‑Conserve limited metal ore resources
‑Save large amount of energy (metal extraction from ores consumes huge energy)
‑Reduce environmental damage from mining and waste disposal

Disadvantages:
‑Cost of collection, sorting and transportation of scrap metals
‑Recycled metals may contain impurities

2. Reduce the use of metals: Use alternative materials (e.g. plastic, glass instead of metal containers)
3. Prevent corrosion of metals (e.g. painting, galvanising iron to stop rusting)
4. Improve industrial processing to reduce waste during metal extraction.

## DSE common misconceptions
1. ❌ All metal oxides can be reduced by carbon
✅ Only metals less reactive than aluminium can be reduced by carbon; aluminium oxide cannot be reduced by carbon.
2. ❌ Extraction of aluminium uses aqueous aluminium oxide electrolysis
✅ Molten aluminium oxide (mixed with cryolite) is used; water will be electrolysed instead if using aqueous solution.
3. ❌ Gold needs chemical extraction
✅ Gold exists as native metal, extracted by physical separation only.
