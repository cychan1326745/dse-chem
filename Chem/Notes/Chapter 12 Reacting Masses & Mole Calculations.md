# Chapter 12 Reacting Masses & Mole Calculations
## 12.1 The mole and Avogadro constant
### Key content
- 1 mole = $6.02\times10^{23}$ particles (atoms, molecules, ions)
- Formula:
$$n=\frac{N}{N_A}$$
$n$ = moles, $N$ = number of particles, $N_A=6.02\times10^{23}\ \text{mol}^{-1}$

### Practice question
Calculate the number of moles in $1.204\times10^{24}$ carbon atoms.

### Solution
$$n=\frac{N}{N_A}=\frac{1.204\times10^{24}}{6.02\times10^{23}}=2.00\ \text{mol}$$

---

## 12.2 Molar mass
### Key content
Molar mass ($M$) = mass of 1 mol substance, unit: $\text{g mol}^{-1}$
$$n=\frac{m}{M}$$
$m$ = mass (g)

### Practice question
Find the mass of 0.50 mol $\text{O}_2$. ($M(\text{O}_2)=32.0\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$)

### Solution
$$m=n\times M=0.50\times32.0=16.0\ \text{g}$$

---

## 12.3 Percentage by mass of an element in a compound
### Key formula
$$\% \text{mass of element}=
\frac{\text{Total mass of the element in 1 mol compound}}{\text{Molar mass of compound}}\times100\%$$

### Practice question
Calculate the % mass of oxygen in $\text{CO}_2$.
$M(\text{C})=12,\ M(\text{O})=16$

### Solution
$$M(\text{CO}_2)=12+2\times16=44\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$$
$$\% \text{O}=\frac{2\times16}{44}\times100\%=\frac{32}{44}\times100\%\approx72.7\%$$

Chemical equation: $\boldsymbol{\text{C}+2\text{O}\rightarrow\text{CO}_2}$

---

## 12.4 Mass of element from mass of compound
### Key idea
Mass of element = mass of compound × percentage mass of that element

### Practice question
Find the mass of carbon in 22 g of $\text{CO}_2$.

### Solution
$$\text{Mass of C}=22\times\frac{12}{44}=6.0\ \text{g}$$

---

## 12.5 Relative atomic mass from isotopes
### Formula
$$A_r=\sum(\text{isotope mass}\times\text{abundance fraction})$$

### Practice question
Chlorine has two isotopes: $\ce{^{35}Cl}$ (75%) and $\ce{^{37}Cl}$ (25%). Calculate $A_r(\text{Cl})$.

### Solution
$$A_r=35\times0.75+37\times0.25=35.5$$

## 12.6 Empirical formula
### Steps
1. Mass → moles
2. Divide by the smallest mole value
3. Get simplest whole number ratio

### Practice question
A compound contains 4.8 g Mg and 3.2 g O. Find its empirical formula.
$M(\text{Mg})=24,\ M(\text{O})=16$

### Solution
$$n(\text{Mg})=\frac{4.8}{24}=0.2\ \text{mol}$$
$$n(\text{O})=\frac{3.2}{16}=0.2\ \text{mol}$$
Ratio $\text{Mg}:\text{O}=1:1$
Empirical formula: $\boldsymbol{\text{MgO}}$

Equation: $\boldsymbol{2\text{Mg}+\text{O}_2\rightarrow2\text{MgO}}$

---

## 12.7 Molecular formula
### Relationship
$$\text{Molecular formula}=(\text{Empirical formula})_n$$
$$n=\frac{\text{Molar mass of molecule}}{\text{Empirical formula mass}}$$

### Practice question
Ethene has empirical formula $\text{CH}_2$, molar mass = $28\ \text{g mol}^{-1}$. Find molecular formula.
$M(\text{CH}_2)=14$

### Solution
$$n=\frac{28}{14}=2$$
Molecular formula: $\boldsymbol{\text{C}_2\text{H}_4}$

## 12.8 Reacting masses from chemical equations
### Conservation of mass
Besides the mole ratio, the chemical equation also tells us information about the reacting masses.

Example:
$$\boldsymbol{2Mg(s) + O_2(g) \rightarrow 2MgO(s)}$$
- $2\;\text{mol} \times 24.3\;\text{g mol}^{-1} = 48.6\;\text{g}$ of magnesium atoms
- react with $1\;\text{mol} \times 32.0\;\text{g mol}^{-1} = 32.0\;\text{g}$ of oxygen molecules
- to form $2\;\text{mol} \times 40.3\;\text{g mol}^{-1} = 80.6\;\text{g}$ of formula units of magnesium oxide

$$48.6\;\text{g} + 32.0\;\text{g} = 80.6\;\text{g}$$

> Law of conservation of mass
Mass is neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction.
Total mass of reactants = Total mass of products.

### Principle
The balanced equation gives the mole ratio of reactants and products.

### Standard calculation workflow
1. Write balanced chemical equation
2. State mole ratio from equation
3. Calculate moles of the substance with known mass
4. Use mole ratio to find moles of target substance
5. Convert moles to mass ($m=n\times M$)

### Calculations involving limiting reactant
Take the reaction between hydrogen and oxygen to form water:
$$2\text{H}_2(\text{g}) + \text{O}_2(\text{g}) \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l})$$

The balanced equation shows the mole ratio:
2 molecules (2 mol) of $\text{H}_2$ react completely with 1 molecule (1 mol) of $\text{O}_2$.

#### Concept
- **Limiting reactant**: the reactant that is completely used up first. It limits the maximum amount of product formed, and the reaction stops once it runs out.
- **Excess reactant**: the reactant that remains unreacted after the reaction finishes.

#### Particle example
If we start with 2 molecules of $\text{H}_2$ and 2 molecules of $\text{O}_2$:
- All $\text{H}_2$ is consumed;
- Only 1 molecule of $\text{O}_2$ is used, leaving 1 molecule of $\text{O}_2$ unreacted.

Here, $\boldsymbol{\text{H}_2}$ is the limiting reactant, and $\boldsymbol{\text{O}_2}$ is in excess.

#### DSE procedure to find limiting reactant
1. Calculate the number of moles of every reactant available.
2. Use the mole ratio from the balanced equation to work out how much of the other reactant each substance can react with.
3. The reactant that runs out first is the limiting reactant.
4. Only use the moles of the limiting reactant to calculate the yield of product.

#### Practice calculation question
0.4 mol $\text{H}_2$ reacts with 0.3 mol $\text{O}_2$.
(i) Identify limiting reactant.
(ii) Moles of water formed.

#### Solution
Mole ratio $\text{H}_2:\text{O}_2=2:1$
0.4 mol $\text{H}_2$ only needs $0.4\div2=0.2$ mol $\text{O}_2$.
We have 0.3 mol $\text{O}_2$, so $\boldsymbol{\text{O}_2}$ is excess; $\boldsymbol{\text{H}_2}$ is limiting.

Moles of $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ = moles of limiting $\text{H}_2$ = $\boldsymbol{0.4\ \text{mol}}$.

### Percentage yield
$$
\text{Percentage yield}
= \frac{\text{Actual yield}}{\text{Theoretical yield}} \times 100\%
$$

#### Yield question
Theoretical mass of $\text{MgO}=20.15\ \text{g}$; actual mass = 16.12 g. Calculate % yield.

#### Solution
$$\% \text{yield}=\frac{16.12}{20.15}\times100\%=80\%$$
